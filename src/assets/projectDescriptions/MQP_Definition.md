The MQP, or Major-Qualifying-Project, is the senior year capstone project that all WPI students have to complete in order to graduate. To complete a successful MQP students must identify a problem in the world that they can use their degree to address. Like more advanced graduate projects, MQPs are meant to drive students to form new, innovative solutions using what they've learned, not just repeat projects they completed in other coursework. MQPs start at the end of Junior year, where students identify their MQP topic, form teams, and petition professors to advise them throughout the process. During senior year, students take two classes a term instead of three, with the MQP replacing the workload of the third class. From September-April, students must maintain a rigid timeline, conducting immense research on their chosen topic, creating multiple prototypes, and putting their project through rigorous testing. In April the project concludes with project presentation day, where students present their work to family members, judges, and members of the faculty and have the opportunity to win prizes based on the success and real-world-applications of their work. Students must create a poster and video showcasing their work as well as a full engineering design paper accounting their research, design process, and results.
